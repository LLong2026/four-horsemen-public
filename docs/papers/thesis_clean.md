# Predictive Maintenance Scheduling for Small Manufacturers

## Abstract

This study evaluates a scheduling policy for predictive maintenance in shops with fewer than fifty machines. We report whether a rolling-horizon policy reduced unplanned downtime in the studied sample.

## 1. Introduction

Predictive maintenance has received significant attention since deep learning entered the field [1]. Earlier structural work established physical constraints that sensor design respects [2]. Time series classification informs our encoding choice [4].

## 2. Method

We compared two policies across 12 shops over six months, summarized in Table 1. The instrument is described in Appendix A.

Unplanned downtime fell from 41 minutes to 32 minutes, a reduction of 22%. The treatment group completed 1,240 of 1,600 orders (77.5%).

Table 1. Outcomes by group.

| Outcome | Control | Treatment | Percent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Downtime hours | 210 | 162 | 44.0% |
| Rework rate | 12 | 9 | 21.0% |
| Throughput index | 100 | 112 | 35.0% |

## 3. Discussion

Within this sample the rolling-horizon policy was associated with lower unplanned downtime. The limits of the design are sample size and a single region. Section 3 covers threats to validity.
